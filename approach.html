How I work

The habits behind my work: how I take on a new problem, how I test, how I use AI, and what I ask a new team. Distilled from my own notes.

01 / Starting on a new problem

  1. Name the problem first: the one business or operational problem the work exists to solve, the skills underneath the tool list, what success looks like at 90 days, and the organisation's current priorities.
  2. One version of every number. I keep a ledger of every metric and claim I make, so a figure never changes between conversations.
  3. Bank what I learn. Every question I'm asked, every story, every technical concept goes into a running knowledge bank, so preparation compounds instead of starting over.

02 / How I test

  • Standardise, then automate, then add AI with human gates: the derivation method first, automation on top, AI only where a human reviews the output.
  • Cheapest layer that catches the fault first: fault list → SiL in CI → log replay → a small HiL with only the real safety controller and one actuator → vehicle last.
  • Prove the tests can fail: seed bugs and check each one is caught before trusting a green run.
  • Check the bench before raising a defect: wrong software, no power or a bad harness look like product failures.
  • A new method earns its place on real work: a short, strict trial on two or three functions settles a debate faster than more meetings.

03 / How I use AI

  • Bounded: AI drafts specs, RCAs and documents; it never decides a verdict or signs anything off.
  • Honest about authorship: I define the problem and what "done" means; the AI tool writes much of the code; I can explain every part.
  • Demo the simplest version that makes the point; keep the advanced versions for "what would you improve?"

04 / Ideas that transfer across domains

05 / What I ask a new team

  • What does success look like for this role at 90 days and at 12 months?
  • What's slowing validation down most right now: environment, restbus, or product?
  • How do results flow into the ALM, and what counts as release evidence?
  • Where would you use AI in validation, and where would you forbid it?
  • Where does the safety check sit in your stack, and is a stop in lane an acceptable final fallback?
